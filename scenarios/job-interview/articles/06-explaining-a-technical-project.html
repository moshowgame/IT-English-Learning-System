Explaining a Technical Project

Article 06 · Job Interview English · 讲清一个技术项目

⏱ 8 min read 🟡 Intermediate 🎯 The two-layer project pitch: business story first, depth on demand

1. Scenario 场景背景

Technical interview, question three: "Pick a project on your CV and walk me through it." You pick the one you know best — a payment reconciliation engine you built with two colleagues. The interviewer, Ruth, is a principal engineer. She will test one thing: can you size your explanation to your audience?

技术面试第三问："挑一个简历上的项目讲一讲。"你选了最熟的一个——你和两位同事一起做的支付对账引擎。面试官 Ruth 是首席工程师。她要验证的只有一件事：你能否根据听众调整讲解的深度？

The trap is depth-first. Candidates who start with schema diagrams lose the interviewer before the story starts. The professional shape is two layers: a 60-second business layer anyone can follow, then an invitation — "want me to go deeper on the design?" — that lets the interviewer pick the depth.

陷阱是"深度优先"。从表结构图开讲的候选人还没进故事就把面试官讲丢了。职业化的结构是两层：先讲 60 秒任何人都能懂的业务层，然后给一个邀请——"需要我深入讲设计吗？"——把深度选择权交给面试官。

2. Dialogue 英文对话

Ruth (Principal Engineer): Pick a project from your CV and walk me through it — start wherever makes sense to you.从简历里挑一个项目讲讲——从你认为合理的地方开始。
You (Candidate): I\'ll do the payment reconciliation engine. The one-sentence version: our bank moved about two million transactions a day between three systems, and roughly 900 of them failed to match every night — each one a manual investigation.我讲支付对账引擎。一句话版本：我们银行每天在三个系统间流动约两百万笔交易，每晚大约 900 笔对不平——每一笔都要人工排查。
You (Candidate): The business problem: those 900 mismatches cost the operations team about 60 person-hours a week, and the backlog grew faster than the team. We automated the matching, cut the daily mismatches to under 40, and gave the remaining ones a triage UI.业务问题：这 900 笔对不平每周消耗运营团队约 60 个人时，且积压增长快于人手。我们自动化了匹配，把每日不平降到 40 笔以下，并给剩余的做了分诊界面。
You (Candidate): My part: I designed the matching pipeline and the exception model. My two colleagues built the UI and the reconciliation rules engine. Want me to go deeper on the matching design, the rollout, or the failure modes?我的部分：匹配管道和异常模型的设计。两位同事负责界面和对账规则引擎。要我深入讲匹配设计、上线过程，还是故障模式？
Ruth (Principal Engineer): Matching design. And don\'t spare the detail.匹配设计。细节不用省。
You (Candidate): The core insight: 98% of mismatches are not mysterious — they are ordering and timing. So the pipeline runs in passes. Pass one: exact match on ID, amount, currency — clears 90% in minutes. Pass two: fuzzy match within a tolerance window — amount within a cent, timestamp within ten minutes. That clears most of the rest.核心洞察：98% 的不平并不神秘——只是顺序和时间差。所以管道分多轮跑。第一轮：ID、金额、币种精确匹配——几分钟内清掉 90%。第二轮：容差窗口内模糊匹配——金额差一分钱内、时间差十分钟内。又清掉大部分。
You (Candidate): Pass three is the part I am proud of: instead of more rules, the leftovers get a feature vector — counterparty, hour-of-day, failure history — and we rank them by likelihood of being the same transaction. The ops team works the ranked list, top first. Human attention became the scarce resource we optimised for.第三轮是我最自豪的：剩余的不再堆规则，而是生成特征向量——对手方、时段、失败历史——按"是同一笔交易的可能性"排序。运营团队按排名处理，从最像的开始。人的注意力成了我们优化的稀缺资源。
Ruth (Principal Engineer): What broke? Tell me about a failure mode you did not expect.出了什么问题？讲一个你没预料到的故障模式。
You (Candidate): Time zones. Pass two\'s tolerance window silently matched transactions across a daylight-saving boundary — same cent, ten minutes apart, wrong pair. It produced 12 false matches before we caught it. The fix taught me more than the build: every tolerance now carries the calendar it was computed under, and the audit log can replay any match decision.时区。第二轮的容差窗口在夏令时边界上静默配对了交易——金额一致、时间差十分钟、但配错了。发现前产生了 12 笔错误匹配。修复比建设教得更多：现在每个容差都带着计算它所用的日历，审计日志可以回放任何一次匹配决定。
Ruth (Principal Engineer): Good depth, and good honesty. Last thing — what would you build differently today?深度不错，态度也诚实。最后一个问题——今天重做你会怎么改？
You (Candidate): Two things. I would start with the audit log, not bolt it on later — replay turned out to be the feature ops actually loved. And I would question pass two earlier: we shipped it on day one because it was easy, but "easy to build" is not a reason, it is just a fact about building.两点。审计日志会从第一天就做，而不是后来补——回放才是运营真正爱的功能。另外我会更早质疑第二轮：第一天就上线它只因为它简单，但"容易实现"不是理由，只是关于实现的事实。

3. Key Phrases 关键句型

  • Layer The one-sentence version: … 一句话版本：…… Earns the right to continue. If the one sentence lands, everything after it is listened to.
  • Business Cost the operations team about 60 person-hours a week. 每周消耗运营团队约 60 个人时。 Translate tech into person-hours and money — this is the language that gets projects funded.
  • Offer Deeper on the design, the rollout, or the failure modes? 深入讲设计、上线，还是故障模式？ The menu question — hands depth control to the interviewer and shows you know there are layers.
  • Insight 98% of mismatches are not mysterious — they are ordering and timing. 98% 的不平并不神秘——只是顺序和时间差。 One insight sentence before the mechanics makes all mechanics feel purposeful.
  • Ownership My part: I designed… My colleagues built… 我的部分：我设计……同事负责…… Credit-sharing that still draws a precise line around your contribution.
  • War story The fix taught me more than the build. 修复比建设教得更多。 Failure modes asked and answered with a lesson — principal engineers score this heavily.
  • Growth "Easy to build" is not a reason, it is just a fact about building. "容易实现"不是理由，只是关于实现的事实。 A portable engineering aphorism — ending on one marks seniority.

4. Vocabulary 词汇表

  • Reconciliation n. 对账 Matching records across systems to confirm they agree.
  • Mismatch n. 对不平 A record pair that should match but does not.
  • Triage n. 分诊 Sorting items by priority so attention goes to the worst first.
  • Pass n. 轮（处理轮次） One sweep of matching logic over the data; pipelines run in passes.
  • Tolerance window n. 容差窗口 The allowed difference (amount, time) within which records may match.
  • Feature vector n. 特征向量 A set of attributes describing an item for ranking or matching.
  • False match n. 错误配对 Two records paired that are not actually the same — worse than none.
  • Daylight-saving boundary n. 夏令时边界 Clock-change dates that break time-based logic. Classic.
  • Audit log n. 审计日志 The record of who did what when — in banks, a first-class feature.
  • Rollout n. 上线推广 The staged release of a system into production.

5. Grammar Notes 语法点

① So + result clause sells the design

"So the pipeline runs in passes.", "so this stopped being…", "so I came prepared…" — so is the interview engineer\'s favourite connective: [insight], so [design]. It makes every mechanical choice feel derived rather than memorised: "98% are ordering and timing, so the pipeline runs in passes." Say the insight first, then the so-clause, and the design explains itself.

so 是面试工程师最爱的连接词：[洞察]，so [设计]。"98% 是顺序和时间差，so the pipeline runs in passes." 让每个机械选择都显得是推导出来的而非背出来的。先说洞察，再说 so 从句，设计自会说明自己。

② Cleft sentences spotlight the proud part

"Pass three is the part I am proud of.", "replay turned out to be the feature ops actually loved." — the [X] is the [thing] that… pattern points the interviewer\'s attention deliberately. Use one per project: mark the component you want questions about. Interviewers follow spotlights; give them yours.

"Pass three is the part I am proud of."、'replay turned out to be the feature ops actually loved.'——[X] is the [thing] that… 句式刻意引导面试官的注意力。每个项目用一次：标出你希望被追问的组件。面试官会跟随聚光灯；把灯打在你想要的地方。

6. Cultural Tips 文化 / 职场 Tips

  • Business layer first, always. 60 seconds: what it was, what it cost, what changed. Diagrams come after the invitation.
  • Offer a menu, not a monologue. "Deeper on design, rollout, or failure modes?" respects the interviewer\'s time and scores communication.
  • Have the war story ready. "What broke?" is a certainty in technical interviews. The daylight-saving bug is a better answer than "nothing major".
  • Claim your slice precisely. "I designed the pipeline; my colleagues built the UI" is honest and strong. Blurry "we" answers invite suspicion.
  • End on a grown-up aphorism. "Easy to build is not a reason" — one sentence that says you reflect by default.

7. Practice 练习题

Exercise 1 — Two-layer your own project

Take your best project. Write the one-sentence version, the 60-second business layer (problem, cost, outcome with a number), and a three-item depth menu.

Test: could a non-engineer follow the business layer? Does the menu name the three parts you could talk about for ten minutes each? If the menu is empty, you are not ready to present this project.

Exercise 2 — Cost translation

Translate these into business language: (a) "p95 latency 400ms", (b) "30% test coverage gap", (c) "manual deployment takes 2 engineers 4 hours".

(a) "checkout feels slow enough that we lose conversions at peak"; (b) "changes ship with avoidable regressions that hit production monthly"; (c) "every release burns 8 person-hours and takes a change-freeze window". Numbers become hours, money, risk.

Exercise 3 — War-story slot

For your project, write the three-sentence answer to "what broke?" — bug, impact, and the lesson that changed how you build.

Structure: "Time zones silently matched across the DST boundary — 12 false matches before we caught it. Every tolerance now carries its calendar, and any decision can be replayed. The fix taught me more than the build." Bug in one line, mechanism in one line, growth in one line.
