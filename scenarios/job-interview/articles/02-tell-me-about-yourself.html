"Tell Me About Yourself"

Article 02 · Job Interview English · 请介绍一下你自己

⏱ 8 min read 🟢 Beginner-friendly 🎯 The 90-second opener: present → past → future, ending in why this role

1. Scenario 场景背景

You are in the first technical interview for a Developer role. It is on Microsoft Teams, camera on. The interviewer, Dan, an engineering manager, joins on time, exchanges hellos, and opens with the question everyone dreads: "So, tell me about yourself."

你在应聘 Developer 岗位的第一轮技术面。面试在 Teams 上进行，摄像头打开。面试官 Dan（工程经理）准时加入，寒暄之后抛出那个所有人都会紧张的问题："请介绍一下你自己。"

This is not a personality question — it is a structure test. Dan wants to see whether you can organise two minutes of information about yourself without rambling. The proven shape is present → past → future: what you do now, the road that got you here, and why this role is the logical next step.

这不是性格问题，而是结构测试。Dan 想看你是否能有条理地组织关于自己的两分钟信息。经过验证的结构是现在 → 过去 → 未来：你现在做什么、什么经历把你带到这里、以及为什么这个岗位是合理的下一步。

2. Dialogue 英文对话

Dan (Engineering Manager): Thanks for making the time today. Let\'s start easy — tell me about yourself.感谢今天抽时间。我们从一个简单的开始——请介绍一下你自己。
You (Candidate): Happy to. Present: I am a backend developer at a mid-size fintech, working on the payments API in a squad of six. I own our interaction with the settlement system — that is the part of the flow I know best.好的。现在：我在一家中型金融科技公司做后端开发，负责支付 API，小队 6 个人。我与清算系统的对接是我最熟悉的部分。
You (Candidate): Past: I started in a service desk role three years ago, moved into development through an internal programme, and have been shipping production code for two years. That path is why I am comfortable talking to both users and engineers.过去：三年前我从服务台岗位起步，通过内部转岗项目进入开发，写生产代码已经两年。这段路径让我既能和用户聊，也能和工程师聊。
You (Candidate): Future: I want to work at bigger scale and higher regulation — which is exactly what your payments platform is. That is why this role, and why now.未来：我想在更大规模、更强监管的环境工作——这正是你们支付平台的特质。所以是这个岗位，也是现在。
Dan (Engineering Manager): Nice structure. What are you strongest at right now?结构很清晰。你现在最擅长什么？
You (Candidate): Two things. Depth: API design and idempotency in payment flows — I have broken and rebuilt enough of them. Breadth: I can talk to a product manager in their language and translate it into tickets, which I think matters a lot in a bank.两点。深度：API 设计和支付流程里的幂等性——我拆过也重建过足够多。广度：我能用产品经理的语言沟通并翻译成工单，这一点在银行里我觉得非常重要。
Dan (Engineering Manager): And the flip side — what are you working on improving?反过来——你正在改进什么？
You (Candidate): Honestly? Frontend depth. I can hold my own in React but I would not call it a strength. I have been doing one internal project a quarter to close that gap — and I would rather fix it visibly than hide it.说实话是前端的深度。React 我能用，但称不上强项。我每季度做一个内部项目来补——我宁愿可见地补齐，而不是藏起来。
Dan (Engineering Manager): Good answer. Tell me what you know about our team.好答案。讲讲你对我们将要做的业务了解多少。
You (Candidate): From the job ad and a colleague who works here: you are rebuilding the real-time balance service, and the pain point is consistency during batch settlement. That sits exactly where my settlement experience is — so I came prepared to talk about that, if useful.从 JD 和一位在这里工作的同事那里了解到：你们在重建实时余额服务，痛点是批量清算期间的一致性。这正好落在我的清算经验范围内——所以我今天是有备而来的，如果需要可以展开。
Dan (Engineering Manager): Very prepared. Let\'s do some technical questions then.准备得很充分。那我们进入技术问题吧。

3. Key Phrases 关键句型

  • Structure Present: … Past: … Future: … 现在：……过去：……未来：…… Saying the signpost words out loud keeps you on track and shows the interviewer the skeleton of your answer.
  • Open Happy to. 好的，很乐意。 A two-word warm start beats "umm, well…". Have it ready.
  • Claim I own our interaction with the settlement system. 我负责与清算系统的对接。 "Own" claims responsibility precisely — stronger than "I worked on".
  • Bridge That path is why I am comfortable talking to both users and engineers. 这段路径让我既能和用户聊也能和工程师聊。 Converts an unusual career path from a question mark into a strength.
  • Strength I can talk to X in their language and translate it into Y. 我能用 X 的语言沟通并翻译成 Y。 Translation is a bank-critical skill — say it explicitly.
  • Honesty I would rather fix it visibly than hide it. 我宁愿可见地补齐，而不是藏起来。 Turning a weakness into a work ethic — interviewers remember this line.
  • Close So I came prepared to talk about that, if useful. 所以我是有备而来的，需要的话可以展开。 Offers depth without hijacking the interview — the interviewer stays in control.

4. Vocabulary 词汇表

  • Squad n. 小队 A small cross-functional delivery team — the standard agile unit in banks.
  • Settlement system n. 清算系统 The system that finalises money movement between institutions.
  • Internal programme n. 内部转岗项目 A structured way employers move staff between roles internally.
  • Idempotency n. 幂等性 The property that repeating an operation has no extra effect — critical in payments.
  • Service desk n. 服务台 First-line IT support — a common entry point into tech careers.
  • Real-time balance service n. 实时余额服务 A service showing a customer's balance as it changes, not end-of-day.
  • Batch settlement n. 批量清算 Processing transactions in scheduled groups — a classic consistency pain point.
  • Interview loop n. 面试流程 All rounds from screening to offer; this is round one of the technical track.
  • Rambling n. 东拉西扯 Talking without structure — the failure mode this question punishes.
  • Denominator n. 分母 What a number is measured against; scores mean little without one.

5. Grammar Notes 语法点

① The present-perfect bridge

"I have been shipping production code for two years." Present perfect (have + past participle) connects your past to the present moment — the tense of "still true now". Use it for tenure, skills and ongoing improvement: "I have been closing the frontend gap one project a quarter." Then switch to past simple the moment you narrate a finished chapter.

现在完成时（have + 过去分词）把过去连到现在——表示"到现在仍然成立"。工龄、技能、持续的改进都用它："I have been shipping production code for two years." 一旦讲述已完结的一章，立刻切换为一般过去时。

② Fronting for structure

Notice the opener pattern: "Present: I am…", "Past: I started…", "Future: I want…". Putting the signpost word first (fronting) lets the interviewer file your answer in real time. It feels mechanical on paper and sounds organised in the room — keep the signposts, vary the phrasing ("These days… / Before that… / Next, I want…") once you are comfortable.

注意开场模式："Present: …"、"Past: …"、"Future: …"。把路标词放句首（前置）让面试官能实时归档你的回答。纸面上显得机械，现场却显得有条理——熟练后可以换说法（These days… / Before that… / Next, I want…），但保留路标。

6. Cultural Tips 文化 / 职场 Tips

  • 90 seconds, not 5 minutes. If the interviewer wants more, they will pull — and pulling is engagement, which is good.
  • End in the role. "…which is why this role and why now" lands the answer in the interview, not in your biography.
  • One weakness, actively managed. A weakness with a closing plan reads as self-awareness; a weakness without one reads as a risk.
  • Research the pain point. Knowing the team\'s current problem and offering to talk about it is the single strongest move in an opener.
  • Practise aloud, not in your head. This answer is muscle memory. Record it once a week during a job hunt.

7. Practice 练习题

Exercise 1 — Build your three beats

Write your own Present / Past / Future beats in three sentences each, ending with "which is why this role and why now."

Quality check: does Present name your role and one thing you own? Does Past explain the road, not list jobs? Does Future connect to THIS role? If Future could be pasted into any interview, it is too generic — name the bank, the platform, or the problem.

Exercise 2 — Fix the rambling answer

Restructure this 4-minute ramble into the 90-second shape.

"Well, I was born in Chengdu and studied mechanics, though actually my first job was in sales, which my cousin arranged… then I did a bootcamp, that was 2021 I think, and now I code, mainly… whatever needs doing really, my manager says I am flexible…"

Keep only three facts: present role ("I am a backend developer at X"), one past bridge ("I moved from sales through a bootcamp in 2021"), future link ("I want to build payment systems at banking scale — this role is exactly that"). Everything else waits for a follow-up question.

Exercise 3 — Your turn

Say your "tell me about yourself" answer aloud twice — once with signpost words, once without. Which sounded more like you? Keep that version, but rehearse the other in case the interviewer interrupts mid-structure.

There is no wrong choice: signposts on = maximum clarity for panel/interviewers taking notes; signposts off = more conversational for informal chats. The professional move is owning both.
