Following Up on the Timeline

Article 14 · Job Interview English · 进度跟进

⏱ 6 min read 🟢 Beginner-friendly 🎯 Chase politely past the silence: date-anchored, low-pressure, doors open

1. Scenario 场景背景

The interview was ten days ago. Grace said, "Decision this week, feedback by Friday." It is now the following Wednesday. Two more days of silence and you will start imagining things. The follow-up email you write today is a work sample too — banks notice how people chase.

面试是 10 天前的事。Grace 说过："本周定，周五前反馈。"现在已是下周三。再沉默两天你就要开始胡思乱想了。今天要写的跟进邮件同样是一份工作样本——银行会注意一个人怎么"催"。

The craft is chasing without pressure: anchor to their date, not your anxiety; ask for a status, not a verdict; and keep every door — including the "you were slow" door — firmly closed in the text.

技巧在于"催而不压"：锚定他们的日期，而不是你的焦虑；问的是进度，不是判决；并且把所有情绪（包括"你们太慢了"）牢牢挡在文本之外。

2. Dialogue 英文对话

You (Candidate): Draft rules, before I write. Rule one: anchor to their date. Grace said Friday — so my first line references that promise, not my mood: "you mentioned feedback by last Friday."动笔前先立规矩。规则一：锚定他们的日期。Grace 说过周五——所以第一句引用那个承诺，而不是我的心情："你提到上周五会给反馈。"
You (Candidate): Rule two: ask for status, not verdict. "Is the process still on track, and is there anything useful from my side?" — the second half makes it easy to reply with a task instead of a decision. Recruiters answer emails that contain a small ask; they avoid emails that demand one.规则二：问进度，不要判决。"流程是否仍在推进，我这边有什么能帮上忙的吗？"——后半句让对方可以回一个小任务而不是一个决定。招聘者会回复包含小请求的邮件；会回避索要决定的邮件。
You (Candidate): Rule three: one channel, one message. No LinkedIn nudge, no second email tomorrow, no call. Silence for five working days after this one — then one more, then I redirect my energy to other loops. Desperation has a smell; discipline is the cologne.规则三：一个渠道，一条信息。不领英私信、明天不补发、不打电话。这条之后再静默五个工作日——然后最多再一条，之后把精力转向其他流程。绝望有味道；纪律才是香水。
You (Candidate): The email itself. Subject: "Following up — [Role] interview, [date]" — the role in the subject because Priya runs twelve loops and memory is a kindness. Body, four sentences: recall the date, reference their timeline, status question with the small ask, warm close that re-states interest once.邮件本身。主题："Following up — [岗位] 面试，[日期]"——主题里带岗位，因为 Priya 同时管着十二个流程，帮对方省记忆是一种善意。正文四句：回顾日期、引用他们的时间线、带小请求的进度提问、重申一次兴趣的温暖收尾。
You (Candidate): Read before sending, in Priya\'s voice: does this email make my day easier or harder? Four sentences, zero guilt, one small ask. Send — and close the laptop.发送前用 Priya 的语气读一遍：这封邮件让我的今天更轻松还是更沉重？四句话、零愧疚感、一个小请求。发送——然后合上电脑。

3. Key Phrases 关键句型

  • Anchor You mentioned feedback by last Friday. 你提到上周五会给反馈。 Their promise, quoted back neutrally — facts without accusation.
  • Ask Is the process still on track? 流程是否仍在推进？ "Still on track" assumes a process, not a verdict — status, not pressure.
  • Enable Is there anything useful from my side? 我这边有什么能帮上忙的吗？ The small ask that makes replying easy — references, documents, a slot.
  • Cadence One channel, one message, five working days. 一个渠道、一条信息、五个工作日。 The follow-up cadence — discipline the recruiter can feel through the screen.
  • Close The role remains very attractive to me. 这个岗位对我仍然非常有吸引力。 Interest re-stated exactly once — enough to warm, not enough to weigh.
  • Mindset Desperation has a smell; discipline is the cologne. 绝望有味道，纪律才是香水。 A memorable one-liner — write it somewhere you will see it during the silence.

4. Vocabulary 词汇表

  • On track phr. 按计划推进 Process moving as planned — the status you are asking about.
  • Loop n. 招聘流程 One hiring process; good candidates run several in parallel.
  • Nudge n. 轻推 A low-value repeated ping — what you must not do.
  • Cadence n. 节奏 The rhythm of your follow-ups: one message, then patience.
  • Subject line n. 邮件主题 Where recall lives: role, date — kindness to a busy inbox.
  • Guilt-trip n. 愧疚绑架 Pressuring through blame — the fastest way out of a shortlist.
  • Shortlist n. 候选名单 The small set still in contention; protect your place in it.
  • Silence n. 沉默 Not a verdict. It is logistics, holidays, and busy calendars.

5. Grammar Notes 语法点

① Present perfect for the waiting that has happened

"I interviewed on the 12th and have not heard back since" — but the professional version stops at "I interviewed on the 12th, and you mentioned feedback by Friday." Present perfect ("have not heard") inserts your waiting into their face; the past-simple + their-promise version inserts only the facts. Same information, opposite temperature. Delete the have-not-heard sentence from every follow-up you write.

"I interviewed on the 12th and have not heard back since"——而职业版本到"I interviewed on the 12th, and you mentioned feedback by Friday"为止。现在完成时（have not heard）把你的等待怼到对方面前；过去时 + 引用承诺的版本只放事实。信息相同，温度相反。把"至今未复"那句从每封跟进邮件里删掉。

② Indirect questions lower the temperature

"Could you let me know whether the process is still on track?" — the whether-clause makes the question about state, not about blame. Compare the direct "Did I fail to get the job?" Direct interrogatives demand verdicts; indirect whether-clauses request updates. Follow-ups live entirely in indirect territory.

"Could you let me know whether the process is still on track?"——whether 从句让问题关于状态而非追责。对比直接问句"我是不是没戏了？"直接疑问句索要判决；间接 whether 从句请求更新。跟进邮件全程生存在间接语域里。

6. Cultural Tips 文化 / 职场 Tips

  • Wait until their date passes — Friday promise means Monday email, not Friday morning. Patience first is what makes the email read calm.
  • Subject line carries the role and date. You are one of twelve loops; make yourself findable in three seconds.
  • Include the small ask. "Anything useful from my side?" converts your follow-up into their to-do item, which is why it gets answered.
  • One follow-up, then five working days. A second message is a status check; a third without a reply is a decision about you — respect it and move energy elsewhere.
  • Keep running other loops. The best follow-up tone comes from candidates who genuinely have alternatives. Build that, not just the email.

7. Practice 练习题

Exercise 1 — Write the four-sentence email

Subject line + four sentences: recall date, reference their timeline, status question with small ask, one-line interest. No sentence longer than 20 words.

Model: "Dear Priya — I interviewed for the Senior BA role on the 12th, and you mentioned feedback by Friday. Could you let me know whether the process is still on track, and whether anything from my side would be useful? References and documents are ready whenever helpful. The role remains very attractive to me. Best, [name]"

Exercise 2 — Temperature check

Which sentence is coldest, and why: (a) "I have not heard back since my interview." (b) "You mentioned feedback by Friday." (c) "I understand processes take time."

(a) — present perfect makes your waiting an accusation in present tense. (b) is neutral fact. (c) is warm but slightly patronising; use (b) plus a small ask instead. The coldest sentences are usually the truest ones — that is why they need the most care.

Exercise 3 — The silence plan

Draft your own cadence rules after sending the follow-up: when is your second message, what is in it, and what redirects your energy meanwhile?

Sensible default: five working days, then one final light message ("still very interested — happy to stay available"); meanwhile two new applications and one skills sprint per week. The plan exists so the silence has an owner: you.
